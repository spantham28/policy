Research briefs

Independent policy briefs written by our student research teams and published open access.

2024

  • Health Policy teamSpring 2024Open access

    Bridging Gaps in Care: Strategic Policy Initiatives for Enhancing Health Care Services for Aging Women

    Evaluates federal and state-level gaps in healthcare delivery for women over 65, with recommendations for Medicare reform, community-based care, and preventive service coverage expansion.

  • Economics & Labor teamSpring 2024Landing page forthcoming

    Federal Homelessness Research: Gaps, Incentives, and a Path Forward

    Identifies critical gaps in federal data collection on homelessness, evaluates Housing First program evidence, and proposes incentive structures to align HUD funding with effective interventions.

2023

  • Technology Policy teamFall 2023Open access

    Accelerating Building Electrification in New England: A Legislative Roadmap

    Examines barriers to residential building electrification across New England and proposes a coordinated regional framework for heat pump adoption and grid modernization.

  • Health Policy teamFall 2023Landing page forthcoming

    Mental Health Screenings and Youth: A Policy Framework for School-Based Intervention

    Analyzes universal mental health screening programs in K–12 schools and proposes a tiered federal framework for school-district implementation.

  • Education Policy teamFall 2023Landing page forthcoming

    Evaluating the Domestic Impact of Returned Peace Corps Volunteers: A Massachusetts Case Study

    Studies the civic and educational impact of returned Peace Corps volunteers as community educators, proposing federal incentives to direct this workforce toward under-resourced school districts.

  • Economics & Labor teamFall 2023Landing page forthcoming

    Gig Work and the Benefits Gap: A Framework for Portable Worker Benefits

    Analyzes structural exclusion of platform workers from employment benefits and proposes a portable benefits model drawing on state-level pilots in New York and Washington.

  • Education Policy teamSpring 2023Landing page forthcoming

    School Resource Officers and the School-to-Prison Pipeline

    Analyzes the evidence on SRO programs and their disproportionate impact on students of color, proposing alternative school safety models grounded in restorative practice.

  • Health Policy teamSpring 2023Landing page forthcoming

    Addressing the Fentanyl Epidemic: Harm Reduction Policy at the State Level

    Examines harm reduction strategies — naloxone access, supervised consumption sites, drug checking services — and evaluates legislative pathways across high-burden states.

  • Technology Policy teamSpring 2023Landing page forthcoming

    Autonomous Vehicles and Liability: Filling the Legislative Gap

    Reviews the patchwork of state AV legislation and proposes a federal baseline framework for liability, insurance, and data-sharing requirements.

  • Economics & Labor teamSpring 2023Landing page forthcoming

    Reshoring Manufacturing: Trade Policy Tools and Their Limits

    Evaluates the economic evidence on domestic manufacturing subsidies, tariff policy, and industrial strategy following the CHIPS Act and IRA, with recommendations for a durable U.S. industrial policy framework.

2022

  • Education Policy teamFall 2022Open access

    Challenges and Opportunities in the COVID-19 Global Education Response

    Assesses how school systems worldwide responded to pandemic disruptions and identifies durable policy reforms for an equitable recovery in global education.

  • Technology Policy teamFall 2022Landing page forthcoming

    Patent Trolling and Innovation: A Reform Agenda for the ITC

    Investigates non-practicing entities' role in suppressing downstream innovation, with targeted reforms to the ITC's Section 337 exclusion order process.

Citing our work

Each brief has a landing page with its abstract, keywords and a suggested citation. Briefs with DOIs registered through Crossref are marked as such; until then, use the landing page address as the stable reference.
